Five AI systems at a (fictional) bank make or shape decisions about people, and nobody inside the bank can explain how they work.

This week I classified every system in my 22-system AI inventory by three things: what it outputs, which algorithm family it uses, and how explainable it is. Then I plotted explainability against how close each system sits to a real decision.

Most generative AI tools landed in the safe-ish corner: staff read the output and decide. The problem corner held five systems:
→ A fraud model that blocks cards automatically
→ A CRM engine that decides which offers customers see
→ A CV screener that ranks candidates (recruiters stop reading at #30)
→ Face matching in digital onboarding
→ An open-source model screening clients for adverse media

Four were bought or embedded. For three, the vendor won't even say what kind of model it is.

Then I mapped the stack. For the bank's own credit model, it controls 5 of 7 layers. For each bought system: 3 of 7. Buying AI doesn't hand over the risk, just the evidence.
